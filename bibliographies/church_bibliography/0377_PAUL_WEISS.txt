377. PAUL WEISS.
                      / . The theory of types. Mind, n. s. vol. 37 (1928), pp. 338-348. "If it were about all
               propositions it would violate the theory of types and be meaningless or self-contradictory.
                If it were not about all propositions, it would not be universally applicable."
                      2. Relativity in logic. The monist, vol. 38 (1928), pp. 536-548.
                      3. The nature of systems. Ibid., vol. 39 (1929), pp. 281-319, 440-472. Reprinted
                Chicago and London, 73 pp. "Though there are a number of systems of mathematics,
                e.g. the different geometries, algebras, etc., and quite a few of logic, e.g. the Boole-Schroe-
                der algebra, the system of material implication and the system of strict implication, there
               are no complete analyses of the nature of systems themselves. . . . It is the purpose of
                the present paper analytically to characterize all possible systems, to justify their method-
                ology, and to attempt a solution of some of the basic problems that arise in dealing with
                them."
                      4. Review of Carnap's Abriss der Logistik. Bull. Amer. Math. Soc, vol. 35 (1929),
                p. 880.
                      5. Entailmenl and the future of logic. Proceedings of the Seventh International Con-
                gress of Philosophy held at Oxford, England, September 1-6, 1930, London 1931, pp.
                143-150. "Accordingly, it is the purpose of this paper (1) to define precisely certain
                widely used but little studied fundamental logical notions; (2) to show how the limitations
                contained in the Principia Mathematica's presentation and analyses are to be transcended,
                and (3) to point the way to future logical study."
                      6. Logic and system. Harvard University, Graduate School of Arts and Sciences,




                Summaries of theses accepted in partial fulfilment of the requirements for the degree of
                Doctor of Philosophy, 1929, Cambridge, Mass., 1931, pp. 217-219.
                     7. Two valued logic—another approach. English, with German abstract by C. G.
                Hempel. Erkenntnis, vol. 2 (1931), pp. 242-261.
                     8. The metaphysics and logic of classes. The monist, vol. 42 (1932), pp. 112-154.
                "A propositional function is defined as an abstraction from a proposition, and both sub-
               jects and predicates are taken to be, in abstraction, propositional functions." "Descriptions
               are treated as identical propositions and the five kinds of identical propositions of the
                Principia Mathematica are reduced to one."
                     9. On alternative logics. The philosophical review, vol. 42 (1933), pp. 520-525. "From
                the fact that there are more than two classes into which all propositions can be grouped,
               or from the fact that the term 'implies' may have many different meanings, it does not
                follow that there are or can be a number of exclusive logics."
