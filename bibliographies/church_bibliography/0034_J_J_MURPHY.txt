34. J. J. MURPHY.
                    1. Relation of logic to language. Belfast Natural History and Philosophical Society,
              Feb. 1875, 21 pp.
                    2. Fundamental logic. Mind, vol. 2 (1877), pp. 47-55.
                    3. On an extension of the ordinary logic, connecting it with the logic of relatives. Memoirs
              of the Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society, 3 s. vol. 7 (1882), pp. 90-101.
              "The present paper has been suggested by the section on Elementary Relatives in Prof.
              Pierce's 'Description of a Notation for the Logic of Relatives.' "
                    4. On the addition and multiplication of logical relatives. Ibid., pp. ?01-224. "On seeing
              the abstract of this paper, Prof. Pierce wrote to me that my addition of relative terms is,
              in all but notation, the same as his internal multiplication of the same. This is quite true."
                    5. On the transformations of a logical proposition containing a single relative term. Ibid.,
              3 s. vol. 8 (1884), pp. 132-138. "The present paper has been suggested by De Morgan's
               'On the Syllogism, No. IV., and on the Logic of Relations.' "
                    6. On the quantification of predicates and on the interpretation of Boole's logical symbols.
              Proceedings of the Literary and Philosophical Society of Manchester, vol. 23 (1884), pp.
              33-36.
                    7. On the meaning of addition and subtraction in logic. Ibid., vol. 25 (1886), pp. 8-16.
                    8. A new system of logical notation. Memoirs and proceedings of the Manchester
              Literary & Philosophical Society, 4 s. vol. 2 (1889), pp. 22-32. "The most important ap-
               plication of this notation is to the 'logic of relatives.' "
                    9. A new symbolic treatment of the old logic. Ibid., 4 s. vol. 4 (1891), pp. 201-215.
               "Every proposition and process known to the old logic may be shown with equal clearness
               without, or with, the use of symbols of relation, but not with equal neatness and concise-
               ness."
