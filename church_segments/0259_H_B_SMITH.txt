                   23. Symbolic logic, problem and method. Photo-lithoprint. 1932, 31 pp. "It is com-
             mon today to distinguish between propositions and propositional functions; the first being
             either true or false for all meanings of the terms that enter into them, the truth or falsity
             of the second depending on what special meanings are assigned to the terms. But we doubt
             if the terminology is worth preserving. We shall mean by a proposition a sentence that is
             either true or false and we shall simply note in any given case whether it is constant or
             variable. The use of the word proposition as currently employed has been unfortunate.
             A 'logic of propositions' is an euphuism used to describe a logic which is not general and
             consequently, a logic which is not true."
