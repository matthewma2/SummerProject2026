335. C. H. LANGFORD.
                     1. Analytic completeness of postulate sets. Proc. London Math. Soc, 2 s. vol. 25
               (1926), pp. 115-142.
                     2. Some theorems on deducibility. Annals of mathematics, 2 s. vol. 28 (1926-7), pp.
                16-40.
                     3. Theorems on deducibility. (Second paper.) Ibid., pp. 459-471.
                     4. On a type of completeness characterizing the general laws for separation of point-pairs.
                Trans. Amer. Math. Soc, vol. 29 (1927), pp. 96-110.
                     5. On inductive relations. Bull. Amer. Math. Soc, vol. 33 (1927), pp. 599-607. "Diffi-
               culties, connected with the occurrence of reflexive fallacies, appear when we attempt to
               give straightforward definitions of inductive series."
                     6. An analysis of some general propositions. Ibid., pp. 666-672.
                     7. On propositions belonging to logic. Mind, n. s. vol. 36 (1927), pp. 342-346. "(1) No
               proposition which is a tautology, in the sense of Wittgenstein, is a proposition certifiable
               on logical grounds alone. This proposition is held to be true because it is a consequence of
               two propositions, jointly, both of which, it will be maintained, are true; namely, (2) No
               proposition which is certifiable on logical grounds alone can have existential import with
               respect to individuals, and (3) Every tautology, in the sense of Wittgenstein, has existen-
               tial import with respect to individuals."
                     8. Singular propositions. Mind, n. s. vol. 37 (1928), pp. 73-81. "The points which I





               wish to consider arise in connexion with a certain analysis of singular propositions, which
               has been suggested recently by Mr. J. A. Chadwick. I wish to raise questions concerning
               singular propositions with regard to their having or lacking existential import."
                   . 9. Concerning logical principles. Bull. Amer. Math. Soc, vol. 34 (1928), pp. 573-582.
               "This means that we cannot have alternative logics; for logic is the system of all proposi-
               tions expressing analytic facts of a certain kind, namely formal analytic facts, and there
               cannot in the nature of the case be more than one such system, actual or conceivable."
                     10. Review of Whitehead and Russell's Principia mathemalica, 2nd. edn., vols. 2
               and 3. Isis, vol. 10 (1928), pp. 513-519.
                     11. Propositions directly about particulars. Mind, n. s. vol. 38 (1929), pp. 219-225.
               "In opposition to this view, I have been maintaining that 'a exists' follows from any
               proposition that is directly about a: and it is these two opposing views that I am going to
               discuss."
                     12. General propositions. Ibid., pp. 436-457. "There are several respects in which an
               account of general propositions of the kind I have in mind would differ from the account
               of these propositions given in Principia Mathemalica, and among these points of difference
               the following is perhaps the most important. I shall try to show how, in constructing gen-
               eral propositions, we can replace elementary matrices, whose values are elementary propo-
               sitions, by functions whose values are not propositions at all, but certain facts, which are
               non-general facts."
                     13. Review of Hilbert and Ackermann's Grundziige der theoretischen Logik. Bull.
               Amer. Math. Soc, vol. 36 (1930), pp. 22-25.
                      14. Otherness and dissimilarity. Mind, n. s. vol. 39 (1930), pp. 454-461.
                      See also C. I. Lewis and C. H. Langford 456.
