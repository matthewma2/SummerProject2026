20. AUGUSTUS DE MORGAN.
         C*           1. Formal logic: or, the calculus of inference, necessary and probable. London 1847,
                xvi-f-336pp.
                      2. On the structure of the syllogism, and on the application of the theory of probabilities
                to questions of argument and authority (read 9 Nov. 1846). Transactions of the Cambridge
                Philosophical Society, vol. 8 (1849), pp. 379-408. "I here venture to propose a derivation
                and classification of the forms of the syllogism, differing very widely from that in use."
                      3. On the symbols of logic, the theory of the syllogism?and in particular of the copula,
                and the application of the theory of probabilities to some questions of evidence (read 25 Feb.
                1850). Ibid., vol. 9 (1856), pp. 79-127.
                      4. Syllabus of aproposed system of logic. London 1860, 72 pp.
                      5. On the syllogism, no. Ill, and on logic in general (read 8 Feb. 1858). Trans. Camb.







               Phil.Soc, vol. 10 (1864), pp. 173-230. "In theopinion of Hegel, we are told, Ploucquet's
               logical calculus was the bitterest libel ever vented against the science. So far as this refers
               to one particular calculus I need say nothing: but, looking on the opinion as one having a
               general direction against symbols, it ought to be noticed that every exact science is only
               so far exact as it knows how to express one thing by one sign. Every science that has thriven
               has thriven upon its own symbols: logic, the only science which is admitted to have made
               no improvements in century after century, is the only one which has grown no symbols."
          **        6. On the syllogism, no. IV, and on the logic of relations (read 23 April 1860). Ibid.,
               pp. 331-358. "In my second and third papers on logic I insisted on the ordinary syllogism
               being one case, and one case only, of the composition of relations. In this fourth paper I
               enter further on the subject of relation, as a branch of logic."
                    7. Appendix. On syllogisms of transposed quantity. Ibid., immediately following the
               preceding, pp. *3SS-*358.
                    8. On the syllogism, no. V., and on various points of the onymatic system (read 4 May
               1863). Ibid., pp. 428-487. .
