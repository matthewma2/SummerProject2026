122. E. V. HUNTINGTON.
          *          1. Sets of independent postulates for the algebra of logic. Transactions of the American
               Mathematical Society, vol. 5 (1904), pp. 288-309.
                    2. A new set of independent postulates for the algebra of logic, with special reference to
               Whitehead and Russell's Principia Mathematica. Proceedings of the National Academy of
               Sciences of the United States of America, vol. 18 (1932), pp. 179-180.
                     3. New sets of independent postulates for the algebra of logic, with special reference to
               Whitehead and Russell's Principia Mathematica. Trans. Amer. Math. Soc, vol. 35 (1933),
               pp. 274-304. See corrections thereto, ibid., pp. 557-558, 971.
                     4. A simplification of Lewis and Langford's postulates for Boolean algebra. Mind, n.s.
               vol. 42 (1933), pp. 203-207.
                    5. The postulational method in mathematics. The American mathematical monthly,
               vol. 41 (1934), pp. 84-92. "The problem of finding a set of postulates for the theory of
              deduction is mathematically just as legitimate a problem as the problem of finding a set
              of postulates for the theory of betweenness or the theory of Boolean algebra or the theory
               of geometry. In each case the postulational problem is a problem of classification; and all
              the postulational technique—consistency, independence, complete independence, etc.—
              appears to be just as applicable in the one case which is not yet satisfactorily solved as it
              is in the other cases where solutions are already familiar."
                    6. Independent postulates for the "informal" part of Principia Mathematica. Bulletin
              of the American Mathematical Society, vol. 40 (1934), pp. 127-136.
                    7. Independent postulates for an "informal Principia system with equality." Ibid.,
              pp. 137-143.
                    8. Independent postulates related to C. I. Lewis's theory of strict implication. Mind,
              n.s. vol. 43 (1934), pp. 181-198.
                    9. The relation between Lewis's strict implication and Boolean algebra. Bull. Amer.
              Math. Soc, vol. 40 (1934), pp. 729-735.
                    10. The inter-deducibility of the new Hilbert-Bernays theory and Principia Mathe-
              matica. Annals of mathematics, 2 s. vol. 36 (1935), pp. 313-324. "A sixteenth example can
              be constructed which provides a formal proof of independence for the rule of inference
              itself."
                    11. Effective equality and effective implication in formal logic. Proc. Nat. Acad. Sci.,
              vol. 21 (1935), pp. 266-271. "The purpose of the present paper is to present an abstract
              mathematical theory which is capable of being interpreted as a part of the calculus of
              propositions—the part, namely, which is expressible in terms of the operation of conjunc-
              tion and the relation of equality, without use of negation or 'truth values.' "
                    12. The mathematical structure of Lewis's theory of strict implication. Fundamenta
              mathematicae, vol. 25 (1935), pp. 147-156.
                    See also E. V. Huntington and Christine Ladd-Franklin 127.
